### ABSTRACT

The **PM Internship Scheme**, launched by the Government of India under the Ministry of Corporate Affairs, aims to provide structured internship opportunities to over one crore young Indians across diverse sectors. However, the existing allocation mechanism relies on basic keyword matching and manual shortlisting, which fails to account for candidate aptitude, geographic accessibility, demographic equity, and fraud prevention.

This project presents **Kaushalya**, an AI-powered internship recommendation engine that replaces traditional matching with a **hybrid machine learning pipeline** combining **content-based filtering** (TF-IDF vectorisation with cosine similarity and semantic skill matching), **collaborative filtering** (Singular Value Decomposition on an interaction matrix), and **affirmative action scoring** (additive boosts for SC/ST/OBC/EWS, rural, and aspirational-district candidates). Additionally, a **Groq-powered Video Introduction Analysis** module transcribes and evaluates candidate communication skills using Whisper and Llama 3.3, providing a dynamic confidence score boost of up to 15%.

The system is built using a **Next.js 14** frontend with Tailwind CSS and Framer Motion, a **FastAPI** backend with SQLAlchemy ORM, **Supabase** (PostgreSQL + cloud storage), and a dedicated **ML engine** (scikit-learn, NumPy, SciPy). A synthetic dataset of **1,000 candidates**, **500 internships**, and **10,000 interactions** â€” generated with census-accurate Indian demographic distributions â€” was used for training and evaluation.

Key features include a **six-step interactive onboarding wizard**, **Aadhaar/DigiLocker identity verification**, an **admin document verification gateway** with a state-machine gating logic, **NLP-based internship fraud detection**, **explainable AI** with per-recommendation match reasons, and a **responsive brutalist UI** with dark mode. The platform is deployed on **Vercel** (frontend) and **Render** (backend) with Supabase as the managed database.

**Keywords:** Recommendation System, Content-Based Filtering, Collaborative Filtering, SVD, Affirmative Action, NLP, Groq, FastAPI, Next.js, PM Internship Scheme.

### 1.1 Background

India, with its rapidly growing young population, faces a significant challenge in bridging the gap between academic education and industry-ready employment. Millions of students graduate every year from universities across the country, yet a vast majority lack practical exposure to real-world professional environments. **Internships** serve as a critical link between theoretical knowledge acquired in classrooms and the practical skills demanded by industry. Recognising this, the Government of India has launched several skilling initiatives aimed at making the youth employable through structured, hands-on training programmes.

Among the most ambitious of these is the **PM Internship Scheme (PMIS)**, announced during the Union Budget 2024â€“25, which envisions providing internship opportunities to **one crore young Indians** over a five-year period across the **top 500 companies** in the country. The scheme is administered by the **Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MoCA)** and carries a government-funded stipend component alongside corporate contributions.

However, the sheer scale of this programme â€” potentially processing millions of candidate profiles against thousands of corporate postings â€” renders traditional manual matching and keyword-based search engines inadequate. A more **intelligent, automated, and equitable** allocation mechanism is urgently needed.

### 1.2 The PM Internship Scheme

The **PM Internship Scheme** was officially launched under the Union Budget 2024â€“25, with the following key parameters:

- **Eligibility:** Indian citizens aged 21â€“24 years who are not employed full-time and not enrolled in full-time education.
- **Duration:** 12 months per internship.
- **Stipend:** â‚¹5,000 per month (â‚¹4,500 from the Government of India + â‚¹500 from the host company).
- **One-Time Grant:** â‚¹6,000 provided at the commencement of the internship.
- **Target:** 1 crore internships over 5 years across 24 identified sectors.
- **Focus Areas:** Special emphasis on candidates from **Scheduled Castes (SC)**, **Scheduled Tribes (ST)**, **Other Backward Classes (OBC)**, **Economically Weaker Sections (EWS)**, **rural areas**, and **aspirational districts**.

The scheme's guidelines explicitly mandate that the allocation system should ensure **fair representation** and **affirmative action** â€” requirements that cannot be met by basic keyword-matching portals.

### 1.3 Motivation

The motivation for this project arises from three critical gaps observed in existing internship allocation platforms:

1. **Lack of Intelligence:** Current portals like Internshala and LinkedIn rely on keyword search and manual filters. They do not understand the *semantic similarity* between skills (e.g., "ReactJS" and "JavaScript Libraries" are related but not textually identical) nor do they consider education-level hierarchies, geographic preferences, or sector alignment in a weighted, mathematical manner.

2. **Absence of Equity Mechanisms:** Existing platforms offer no built-in support for **affirmative action**. A candidate from an aspirational district in rural Bihar competes on identical footing with a candidate from an elite institution in Bangalore. The PM Internship Scheme's guidelines specifically demand equitable representation, which requires *algorithmic* enforcement, not just policy declarations.

3. **Fraud Vulnerability:** Government-backed internship portals have historically been targets for fraudulent postings. Scam listings often use suspicious terms ("processing fee", "guaranteed job"), unrealistic stipends, or free email domains. No existing platform provides automated, **NLP-based fraud detection** for incoming corporate postings.

**Kaushalya** was conceived to address all three gaps in a single, integrated platform.

### 1.4 Objectives

The primary objectives of this project are:

1. To design and implement a **hybrid recommendation engine** that combines content-based filtering, collaborative filtering, and affirmative action scoring to generate personalised internship recommendations.
2. To build a **secure, multi-step verification pipeline** using Firebase OTP authentication and admin-gated document verification to prevent fraudulent candidate registrations.
3. To develop an **AI-powered video analysis module** using Groq's Whisper and Llama 3.3 models that evaluates candidate communication skills from a self-introduction video, providing a dynamic confidence score boost.
4. To implement **NLP-based fraud detection** for internship postings using rule-based and pattern-matching techniques.
5. To ensure **explainable AI** â€” each recommendation must be accompanied by transparent, human-readable reasons explaining *why* the match was made.
6. To build a **responsive, modern web application** with a premium brutalist UI aesthetic, deployable on cloud infrastructure (Vercel + Render + Supabase).

### 1.5 Scope of the Project

The scope of Kaushalya encompasses:

- A **full-stack web application** with a Next.js 14 frontend and a FastAPI Python backend.
- A **machine learning recommendation engine** with content-based and collaborative filtering modules.
- A **synthetic dataset generator** producing 1,000 candidates, 500 internships, and 10,000 interactions with census-accurate Indian demographic distributions.
- An **admin panel** for document verification, dashboard analytics, and model performance monitoring.
- **Cloud deployment** on Vercel (frontend), Render (backend), and Supabase (database and file storage).

### 2.1 Recommendation Systems â€” An Overview

**Recommendation systems** are information filtering tools that predict a user's preference for an item. They are broadly categorised into three paradigms:

- **Content-Based Filtering (CBF):** Recommends items similar to those a user has liked in the past, based on item features. In the context of job recommendations, CBF compares a candidate's skill vector against an internship's requirement vector using similarity measures like cosine similarity [1].
- **Collaborative Filtering (CF):** Recommends items based on the behaviour of similar users. Matrix factorisation techniques, particularly **Singular Value Decomposition (SVD)**, decompose the user-item interaction matrix into latent factors to discover hidden patterns [2].
- **Hybrid Systems:** Combine CBF and CF to overcome their individual limitations (cold-start problem in CF, over-specialisation in CBF). Netflix, LinkedIn, and Spotify all use hybrid approaches [3].

### 2.2 Content-Based Filtering in Job Recommendation

Content-based approaches in job recommendation typically use **TF-IDF** (Term Frequencyâ€“Inverse Document Frequency) vectorisation to convert textual skill descriptions into numerical vectors, followed by **cosine similarity** to measure the angle between candidate and job vectors [4]. More recent approaches use **sentence-transformer embeddings** (e.g., the all-MiniLM-L6-v2 model) to capture semantic meaning, allowing the system to recognise that "Python" and "Data Analysis" are contextually related even without textual overlap [5].

### 2.3 Collaborative Filtering and SVD

**Singular Value Decomposition (SVD)** is a matrix factorisation technique that decomposes a sparse user-item interaction matrix **R** into three matrices: **R â‰ˆ U Ã— Î£ Ã— V^T**, where U captures user latent factors, Î£ captures the strength of each factor, and V^T captures item latent factors. The predicted rating for a user-item pair is obtained by reconstructing the matrix from the top-k singular values [6]. The `scipy.sparse.linalg.svds` implementation was chosen over the `scikit-surprise` library because the latter requires C++ build tools that cause deployment failures on Windows-based cloud servers.

### 2.4 Hybrid Approaches

Research by Burke (2002) established the taxonomy of hybrid recommender systems, identifying seven hybridisation strategies [7]. The **weighted hybrid** approach â€” where content-based and collaborative scores are linearly combined with tunable weights â€” is the most interpretable and widely used in production systems. Our system uses a 60:40 content-to-collaborative weighting for warm users and a 100:0 pure content-based mode for cold-start users (those with fewer than three interactions).

### 2.5 Fairness and Affirmative Action in Recommendations

Algorithmic fairness in recommendation systems has received significant attention. Zehlike et al. (2017) proposed the FA*IR algorithm for fair top-k ranking, ensuring that protected groups receive proportional representation in ranked lists [8]. In the Indian government context, the PM Internship Scheme's guidelines mandate specific representation for SC, ST, OBC, EWS, rural, and aspirational-district candidates. Our implementation uses **additive score boosts** rather than multiplicative factors, ensuring that affirmative action enhances without distorting the relevance signal.

### 2.6 NLP for Skill Extraction and Video Analysis

Whisper, developed by OpenAI, is a large-scale automatic speech recognition (ASR) model trained on 680,000 hours of multilingual data [9]. Groq's hardware-accelerated inference provides real-time transcription at speeds exceeding 200x real-time. For semantic analysis, Meta's Llama 3.3 70B model enables zero-shot evaluation of candidate communication quality from transcribed text, generating structured JSON assessments of clarity, confidence, and speech pace [10].

### 4.1 System Architecture

The Kaushalya platform follows a **three-tier architecture** with clear separation of concerns:

1. **Presentation Layer (Frontend):** A Next.js 14 application using the App Router, styled with Tailwind CSS and animated with Framer Motion. It communicates with the backend exclusively via RESTful API calls using Bearer token authentication.

2. **Application Layer (Backend):** A FastAPI (Python 3.12) server providing 9 API router modules with 30+ endpoints. It handles authentication (JWT), business logic, database operations (SQLAlchemy ORM), file uploads (Supabase Storage), and AI service orchestration (Groq API).

3. **Data & Intelligence Layer:** A managed Supabase PostgreSQL database storing candidates, internships, interactions, recommendations, and documents. The ML engine (a sibling `ml/` Python package) provides the hybrid recommendation scorer, skill taxonomy builder, collaborative filtering trainer, and affirmative action calculator.

### 4.4 ML Pipeline Design

The ML recommendation pipeline operates in the following sequence:

1. **Cold-Start Detection:** Check if the candidate has â‰¥ 3 interactions. If fewer, use pure content-based scoring (weight 1.0). Otherwise, use hybrid mode (content 0.6 + collaborative 0.4).

2. **Content-Based Scoring:** The `ContentBasedEngine` class computes a weighted sum across five dimensions:
   - **Skill Similarity (35%):** Exact match + substring partial match + semantic similarity via `difflib.SequenceMatcher`.
   - **Education Match (20%):** Hierarchical comparison (10TH=1, 12TH=2, ITI=2, DIPLOMA=3, GRADUATE=4, PG=5). Over-qualified = full score.
   - **Location Score (20%):** Same state = 1.0; same region = 0.8; PAN_INDIA = 0.6. Acts as a **hard filter** for HOME_STATE/NEARBY preferences.
   - **Sector Alignment (15%):** Exact match = 1.0; partial = 0.7; no match = 0.2.
   - **Field of Study (10%):** Exact match = 1.0; partial = 0.7; no match = 0.3.

3. **Collaborative Filtering Score:** SVD-predicted score normalised to [0, 1] from the original [1, 5] scale.

4. **Hybrid Combination:** `hybrid_score = (content_weight Ã— content_score) + (cf_weight Ã— cf_score)`

5. **Affirmative Action Boost:** Additive boost based on candidate demographics (see Section 4.6).

6. **Video Communication Bonus:** Up to +10% based on the normalised video_overall_score.

7. **Final Score:** `final_score = min(hybrid + aa_boost + video_bonus, 1.0)`

### 4.6 Affirmative Action Module

The affirmative action module applies **additive** boosts after hybrid scoring, ensuring that advantaged candidates are never penalised.

**Table 4.4: Affirmative Action Boost Values**

| Condition                    | Boost  | Justification                              |
|-----------------------------|--------|--------------------------------------------|
| Aspirational District        | +5%    | From 112 govt-designated districts          |
| SC / ST                      | +4%    | Scheduled Castes and Tribes representation  |
| OBC                          | +2%    | Other Backward Classes representation       |

The module also enforces **diversity constraints**: at most 3 out of 5 recommendations from the same sector, and at most 2 from the same company.

### 4.7 Video Analysis Pipeline

The video analysis pipeline processes candidate self-introduction videos through three stages:

1. **Audio Extraction:** The uploaded MP4/WebM file is processed using `imageio-ffmpeg` to strip the video track and compress the audio to a 64kbps mono MP3 stream. This reduces a 50MB video to approximately 1â€“2MB of audio.

2. **Transcription:** The compressed audio is sent to **Groq's Whisper Large V3 Turbo** model, which returns the full transcript, detected language, and word count.

3. **Semantic Analysis:** The transcript is fed to **Groq's Llama 3.3 70B Versatile** model with a carefully engineered prompt that instructs it to return a structured JSON object containing: `communication_score` (0â€“100), `confidence_score` (0â€“100), `clarity_score` (0â€“100), `overall_score` (0â€“100), `skills_mentioned`, `sectors_mentioned`, `languages_used`, `is_bilingual`, `filler_word_count`, `speech_pace`, `top_strength`, `summary`, `feedback_for_candidate`, and `is_valid_intro`.

### 5.4 Authentication and Verification Pipeline

The system implements a **three-step state machine** using an integer `auth_step` field on the Candidate model:

| auth_step | State                    | Capabilities Unlocked                     |
|-----------|--------------------------|-------------------------------------------|
| 1         | Registered (OTP verified)| Can access wizard, upload documents        |
| 2         | Wizard completed         | Profile fully populated, documents pending |
| 3         | Admin approved           | Full access to ML recommendations          |

The transition from step 2 â†’ 3 occurs **automatically** when all uploaded documents for a candidate are marked "APPROVED" by an admin. The `documents.py` review endpoint performs an atomic check after each approval:

```python
all_docs = db.query(CandidateDocument).filter(candidate_id=...).all()
has_pending = any(d.status != "APPROVED" for d in all_docs)
if all_docs and not has_pending:
    candidate.auth_step = 3  # FULL ACCESS UNLOCKED
```

## CONCLUSION
_____________________________________________________________________

This project successfully demonstrates the design, implementation, and deployment of **Kaushalya**, an AI-powered internship recommendation engine tailored to the specific requirements of the Government of India's PM Internship Scheme. The system addresses the core limitations of existing allocation platforms through a multi-faceted technical approach.

The **hybrid recommendation engine** combines content-based filtering (using TF-IDF vectorisation, hierarchical education matching, geographic proximity scoring, and semantic skill similarity) with collaborative filtering (SVD matrix factorisation on user-interaction data) and affirmative action scoring (configurable additive boosts for SC/ST/OBC/EWS, rural, and aspirational-district candidates). This three-pronged approach ensures that recommendations are simultaneously **relevant**, **equitable**, and **transparent**.

The **video analysis pipeline**, powered by Groq's Whisper and Llama 3.3 models, introduces a novel dimension to candidate assessment â€” evaluating spoken communication skills, confidence, and clarity from a self-introduction video. This provides a dynamic score boost of up to 15%, levelling the playing field for candidates who may have strong oral communication but weaker written profiles.

The **three-step authentication and verification pipeline** (OTP â†’ Wizard â†’ Admin Approval) ensures data integrity and prevents fraudulent applications. The state-machine gating logic (`auth_step`) completely locks out unverified candidates from accessing ML recommendations, enforcing a strict separation between unverified and verified user states.

The **NLP fraud detection module** evaluates incoming internship postings for suspicious patterns, providing an automated first line of defence against scam listings on a government platform.

From an engineering perspective, the project demonstrates competence in:
- **Full-stack web development** (Next.js 14 + FastAPI + PostgreSQL)
- **Machine learning pipeline design** (training, serving, fallback handling)
- **Cloud deployment** (Vercel + Render + Supabase) with real-world constraints (512MB RAM, connection pooling, CORS)
- **Production debugging** (CORS-disguised server crashes, static score bugs, session race conditions)

The platform is deployed and operational, with a responsive UI that works across desktop and mobile devices.
